Made in India, Sold in Dubai: A Legal Roadmap for Indian Manufacturing SMEs Expanding in Dubai

Dubai, with its strategic location, business-friendly environment, and robust infrastructure, is an alluring destination for Indian manufacturing SMEs seeking to expand their global footprint. The sectors of auto parts, textiles, and electronics manufacturing have found particular success in the Emirate. However, a successful expansion requires navigating a complex legal landscape. This guide provides an overview of key legal considerations for Indian manufacturers venturing into Dubai.

The Case of Motherson Sumi Systems Ltd.’s Success in Dubai

Noida-based Indian auto-component multinational Samvardhana Motherson International (SAMIL) (Formerly, Motherson Sumi Systems Ltd. ),,expanded into Dubai with a wiring harness facility for components used in commercial vehicles, that will be exported out to Europe. Bolstered by the Indo-UAE free trade agreement (FTA) to bring itself closer to its European customer, SAMIL nevertheless started with a clear understanding that success in a foreign market hinges on a solid legal foundation. SAMIL encountered various legal challenges specific to the auto parts industry, which they meticulously addressed to ensure a smooth and successful entry into Dubai’s market.

Product Liability & Compliance: MSSL recognized that Dubai’s stringent product liability laws, holding manufacturers responsible for both physical harm and financial losses caused by defective products, demanded meticulous attention to detail. To mitigate risks, MSSL implemented robust quality control processes throughout their manufacturing operations, ensuring that their auto parts met or exceeded the stringent UAE and international safety standards. They also invested in comprehensive product testing to identify and rectify any potential defects before products reached the market.

Import/Export Regulations: Dubai’s complex import/export regulations was another hurdle. MSSL proactively engaged legal counsel to ensure their products complied with all customs requirements, documentation standards, and relevant technical regulations. This proactive approach helped them avoid costly delays and disruptions to their supply chain.

Labor Laws & Workforce Management: MSSL understood the importance of fair and compliant labour practices in Dubai. They ensured their employment contracts aligned with UAE labour laws, covering aspects like wages, working hours, leaves, and termination procedures. By prioritising the well-being and rights of their workforce, MSSL fostered a positive work environment and minimise the risk of labour disputes.

Key Takeaways & Lessons Learned:

MSSL’s experience provides valuable lessons for Indian manufacturing SMEs eyeing Dubai’s market:

  • Proactive Legal Strategy: Engage legal counsel early in the expansion process to identify and address potential legal challenges specific to your industry.
  • Compliance as a Priority: Invest in quality control, product testing, and thorough documentation to ensure compliance with local regulations.
  • Strong Contracts: Draft comprehensive contracts with suppliers, distributors, and employees to protect your interests and clearly define roles and responsibilities.
  • Cultural Sensitivity: Understand and respect local business practices and cultural norms to build strong relationships with partners and stakeholders.

By learning from MSSL’s strategic approach and adopting best practices, Indian SMEs can navigate the legal landscape of Dubai with confidence, mitigate risks, and achieve sustainable success in this lucrative market.

Product Liability & Dubai’s New Consumer Protection Law

Dubai’s 2020 Consumer Protection Law represents a significant shift in the regulatory environment, particularly in the product liability and consumer rights enforcement space. For Indian manufacturers, understanding and adhering to these stricter provisions is crucial to avoid legal complications and foster a positive brand image in the market. The increased stringency is evident in several key areas:

  • Consumers now have stronger rights to seek redress for defective products, misleading practices, and unfair contract terms. This includes the right to refunds, replacements, and compensation for damages, empowering consumers and holding businesses accountable.
  • The law now imposes stricter penalties for non-compliance, including fines of up to AED 2 million and potential imprisonment for certain offences. This heightened penalty structure aims to deter businesses from engaging in unfair or deceptive practices.
  • The law expands the scope of product liability, making manufacturers responsible not only for physical harm caused by defective products but also for financial losses incurred by consumers.
  • The law explicitly recognizes data protection as a consumer right and imposes obligations on businesses to protect consumer data from misuse.

For Indian manufacturers, understanding and adhering to these stricter provisions is paramount to avoid legal pitfalls, financial penalties, and reputational damage. Proactive compliance not only mitigates risks but also fosters consumer trust and loyalty, contributing to long-term success in the Dubai market. Here are the list of things you should keep in mind:

The law expands the scope of product liability, making manufacturers accountable for a wider range of damages caused by their products. This includes:

  • Physical Harm: Injuries or health issues caused by defective products, such as faulty auto parts leading to accidents, allergic reactions to textile dyes, or electrical shocks from malfunctioning electronics.
  • Financial Losses: Monetary damages incurred by consumers due to defective products, such as repair costs for a malfunctioning vehicle, lost income due to a work stoppage caused by faulty equipment, or financial losses incurred due to a data breach caused by insecure software.

For example,

  • If a faulty auto part leads to an accident causing vehicle damage or loss of income due to downtime, the manufacturer could be held liable for those costs.
  • If a textile product causes skin irritation or allergic reactions leading to medical expenses or lost wages, the manufacturer may be liable.
  • If a software glitch in a tech product results in data loss or financial harm to a consumer, the manufacturer could face liability claims.

Dubai’s product liability regime shares similarities with India’s Consumer Protection Act, 2019. Both emphasise consumer protection and hold manufacturers accountable for defective products. However, Dubai’s law imposes stricter penalties and a wider scope of liability, covering both physical and financial damages. Indian manufacturers entering the Dubai market should be aware of these differences and adapt their practices accordingly.

  • Transparency & Disclosure: The law imposes stringent transparency and disclosure requirements on manufacturers, going beyond what is typically mandated in India. These requirements aim to empower consumers with comprehensive information about products, ensuring informed purchasing decisions and holding manufacturers accountable for accurate representation.

The law mandates the disclosure of a wide range of product details, extending beyond basic information like ingredients and usage instructions. This includes:

  • In the automobile industry, detailed specifications of auto parts, including materials used, manufacturing processes, performance metrics, and potential risks associated with improper installation or use.
  • Clear labelling of textile composition, care instructions, potential allergens, and any chemicals used in textile production that could pose health risks.
  • Comprehensive disclosure of software specifications, compatibility issues, potential vulnerabilities, data collection practices, and warranty terms for tech products.
  • Dubai’s law extends transparency requirements to digital products and services, mandating disclosures regarding data collection, privacy policies, and terms of use. This is an area where India’s law is still evolving.
  • Dubai’s law generally requires more explicit warnings and disclaimers regarding potential risks or side effects of products, compared to India.
  • While both Dubai and India require ingredient lists for food products, Dubai’s law often demands more detailed information, including potential allergens and additives.
  • Consumer Complaints: Dubai’s 2020 Consumer Protection Law establishes robust mechanisms for consumers to file complaints against manufacturers for defective products or misleading practices. These mechanisms, such as dedicated consumer courts and online dispute resolution platforms, are designed to ensure swift and fair resolution of consumer grievances. While India’s Consumer Protection Act, 2019 also provides avenues for complaint redressal, the processes in Dubai are often considered more streamlined and efficient. Indian manufacturers must recognize this and implement a proactive approach to addressing consumer complaints in the UAE. A prompt and fair resolution not only helps avoid legal battles but also fosters consumer trust, which is crucial for building a strong brand reputation in this competitive market.
  • Penalties for Non-Compliance: The consequences of violating the Consumer Protection Law in Dubai can be significantly more severe than those under India’s Consumer Protection Act. Fines in Dubai can reach up to AED 1 million (approximately ₹2.2 crore), far exceeding the maximum penalties in India, which typically range from ₹10,000 to ₹50 lakh depending on the nature and severity of the violation. Additionally, Dubai’s law allows for product recalls, suspension of business licences, and even criminal charges in some cases, particularly for repeat offenders or serious violations. This stricter enforcement regime in Dubai underscores the importance of compliance for Indian SMEs. Prioritising consumer protection and adhering to the law not only avoids costly penalties but also safeguards the company’s reputation and long-term success in the UAE market.

Emirates Conformity Assessment Scheme (ECAS)

Dubai’s Emirates Conformity Assessment Scheme (ECAS) is a mandatory product certification system that serves as a critical gateway to the UAE market. While essential for ensuring product safety and quality, ECAS can present a complex maze of regulations and procedures for Indian manufacturers.

Scope: ECAS covers a vast array of products, including electronics, toys, food items, textiles, and construction materials. Indian manufacturers must identify the specific technical regulations and standards applicable to their products. This often requires meticulous research and understanding of UAE-specific requirements, which may differ significantly from Indian standards.

Procedures: The conformity assessment process involves testing, inspection, and certification by accredited Conformity Assessment Bodies (CABs). Navigating this process can be time-consuming and costly, especially for smaller businesses. Legal counsel can streamline this process, ensuring proper documentation and timely approvals.

Technical Regulations: ECAS regulations are extensive and cover diverse aspects like safety, labelling, electromagnetic compatibility, and energy efficiency. These regulations are often more stringent than their Indian counterparts, requiring manufacturers to modify their products or production processes. Legal experts can help interpret and apply these regulations, ensuring compliance and avoiding costly delays.

Labelling & Marking: Products must bear the Emirates Quality Mark (EQM) along with accurate labelling in Arabic and English. This can pose challenges for Indian manufacturers unfamiliar with UAE labelling requirements. Legal advisors can guide businesses through this process, ensuring compliance and avoiding potential penalties.

While both India and the UAE have product certification systems, ECAS in Dubai is generally considered more stringent, with detailed technical regulations and a wider scope of products. Indian manufacturers often face challenges adapting to these stricter standards and complying with the specific requirements of ECAS. Engaging legal counsel with expertise in both Indian and UAE product safety regulations can be instrumental in navigating this complex landscape and ensuring a smooth entry into the Dubai market.

By addressing these challenges with the help of legal professionals, Indian manufacturers can successfully navigate the ECAS process, ensuring their products meet Dubai’s stringent standards and gain consumer confidence.

UAE Labour Law Reforms

The 2022 UAE Labor Law Reforms introduced significant changes that Indian businesses operating in Dubai must understand to ensure compliance and maintain a productive workforce. For instance, the reforms clarified probationary periods, which are now capped at six months, unlike India where it can extend up to a year. Additionally, the UAE law provides greater flexibility in contract types, including part-time and temporary contracts, whereas India primarily focuses on fixed-term and permanent contracts.

Another key difference lies in termination procedures. The UAE law mandates notice periods ranging from 14 days to 90 days, depending on the employee’s length of service, while India’s notice periods can vary significantly based on industry and employment terms.

When it comes to wages and hours, both countries have minimum wage laws, but the UAE law mandates a weekly rest day and limits overtime hours more strictly than Indian law. Moreover, the UAE law stipulates specific end-of-service benefits, including gratuity, which is calculated based on years of service and salary, whereas India’s gratuity system is based on a formula considering an employee’s last drawn salary and tenure.

Dubai’s 2030 Industrial Strategy & New Waste Management Regulations

Dubai’s 2030 Industrial Strategy emphasizes sustainable manufacturing. The 2021 Waste Management Regulations impose stringent requirements on industrial waste disposal and recycling. Indian manufacturers must adopt eco-friendly practices, reduce waste generation, and comply with these regulations to contribute to Dubai’s environmental goals.

Changes to Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) for UAE Companies

The Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), introduced in 2019, are a critical component of Dubai’s efforts to combat harmful tax practices and promote genuine economic activity within its borders. For Indian SMEs setting up shop in Dubai, compliance with ESR is not just a legal requirement but a strategic necessity for long-term success.

What ESR Entails:

  • Substance over Form: ESR mandates that companies conducting “Relevant Activities” in Dubai must demonstrate a substantial economic presence, meaning they must have adequate physical offices, employees, and revenue generated within the UAE.
  • Relevant Activities: These include banking, insurance, investment fund management, lease-finance, headquarters, shipping, holding company business, intellectual property, and distribution and service center business.
  • Notification & Reporting: Companies engaged in Relevant Activities must submit annual notifications to the relevant regulatory authority and provide detailed reports on their economic substance, including information on income, expenses, assets, employees, and core income-generating activities.

Implications for Indian SMEs:

  • Proof of Economic Presence: Indian manufacturers must be prepared to substantiate their economic presence in Dubai by demonstrating that they have the necessary infrastructure, personnel, and financial resources to conduct their business activities. This may involve leasing office space, hiring local employees, and generating revenue within the UAE.
  • Compliance Costs: Compliance with ESR entails additional costs, including the preparation of economic substance reports and potential fees for professional services. Indian SMEs should factor in these costs when planning their expansion into Dubai.
  • Penalties for Non-Compliance: Non-compliance with ESR can result in significant penalties, including financial fines, suspension of business licences, and even blacklisting from doing business in Dubai. It is crucial for Indian manufacturers to take ESR seriously and ensure full compliance to avoid these negative consequences.
  • Seeking Professional Guidance: ESR can be complex, especially for companies new to the Dubai market. Engaging the services of legal and tax professionals specialising in ESR can prove invaluable in ensuring compliance and avoiding costly mistakes.

By proactively addressing ESR requirements, Indian manufacturers can demonstrate their commitment to operating as legitimate businesses in Dubai, thereby strengthening their reputation, mitigating legal risks, and ensuring a smooth and successful expansion into this vibrant market.

As highlighted in this guide, expanding into Dubai’s dynamic market offers immense potential for Indian manufacturing SMEs. However, navigating the intricate legal landscape requires a strategic approach and a keen understanding of local regulations. To ensure a smooth transition and mitigate legal risks, Indian manufacturers should approach lawyers experienced in UAE corporate law, labor law, intellectual property, and international trade. These legal professionals can provide invaluable guidance on navigating the complex Emirates Conformity Assessment Scheme (ECAS), ensuring compliance with stringent consumer protection laws, and adhering to the evolving labor regulations, which often differ significantly from their Indian counterparts. By proactively addressing legal considerations, Indian manufacturers can establish a strong foothold in Dubai, build a trusted brand, and achieve sustainable growth in this vibrant market. Remember, expert legal counsel is not just an expense but an investment in your company’s future success in the UAE.
